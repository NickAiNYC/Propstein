# Week 1

Week 1 is the worst week of the season for this model, and the gap is large
enough that it changes what you should do with the output.

## Measured degradation

Walk-forward, 2022–2025, no Week-1 handling:

| Bucket | n | REC Brier | REC ECE | YDS Brier | YDS ECE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | 606 | 0.2197 | **5.87%** | 0.2410 | **8.54%** |
| Weeks 2–4 | 1,822 | 0.2289 | 1.09% | 0.2449 | 2.31% |
| Weeks 5–9 | 2,873 | 0.2291 | 2.64% | 0.2454 | 0.95% |
| Weeks 10+ | 5,251 | 0.2321 | 0.84% | 0.2455 | 0.44% |

Calibration error is **7× worse for receptions and 19× worse for yards** than
late season. Note the Brier score is *better* in Week 1 — the outcomes are
easier to separate, because the board is full of obvious starters. The model is
sharp and biased at the same time. Brier alone would have hidden this.

## The bias has a direction

| Bucket | REC predicted | REC actual | Bias | YDS predicted | YDS actual | Bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | 0.417 | 0.360 | **+0.057** | 0.509 | 0.421 | **+0.088** |
| Weeks 2–4 | 0.414 | 0.405 | +0.009 | 0.509 | 0.486 | +0.022 |
| Weeks 5+ | 0.406 | 0.409 | −0.004 | 0.500 | 0.497 | +0.003 |

**Week 1 comes in under.** Receivers underperform their carried-forward form by
5.7pp (receptions) and 8.8pp (yards) in over-probability terms. Rust,
conservative early game plans, and unfamiliar personnel all push the same way.

## Correction, validated out-of-sample

Bias constants fitted on Week 1 of 2022–23, tested on Week 1 of 2024–25 (n=306):

| Variant | REC ECE | REC Brier | YDS ECE | YDS Brier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Raw | 6.97% | 0.2156 | 7.98% | 0.2439 |
| **+ bias correction** | **4.88%** | 0.2113 | **2.02%** | 0.2369 |
| + shrinkage only | 6.65% | 0.2111 | 8.13% | 0.2426 |
| + shrinkage + bias | 5.05% | **0.2070** | 3.07% | **0.2349** |

The bias correction generalises and does most of the work. Shrinkage adds
sharpness (best Brier) at a small calibration cost. Both are applied.

> [!WARNING]
> **Line-setter refit caveat:** `W1_BIAS_REC = 0.057` was fitted in the walk-forward backtest against lines generated by the legacy line setter (`ppf(0.5) + 0.5`), which systematically placed reception lines a half-step too high (~43% mean P(over)). With the hook placement now corrected to pick the half-point closest to 50% P(over), `W1_BIAS_REC` likely absorbed some line-placement error and will need refitting post-Week 1. The yards constant (`W1_BIAS_YDS = 0.088`) was continuous and remains completely unaffected.

## The Week 18 bug

Week 18 is systematically unrepresentative — playoff teams rest starters,
eliminated teams play backups. With a 5-game EWMA the most recent game carries
⅓ of the weight, so one meaningless Week 18 cameo wrecks a star's projection.

CeeDee Lamb went 1 target / 4 yards in Week 18 2025. That dragged his 2026 Week
1 projection from ~83 yards (his season average) to **50**. The market had him
near 75.

Fix: for Week 1 only, drop Week 18 and blend the EWMA 50/50 with the
prior-season mean. Measured on Week 1 of 2022–2025, against actual outcomes:

| Carry rule | REC MAE | REC r | YDS MAE | YDS r |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5-game EWMA | 1.573 | 0.516 | 20.99 | 0.531 |
| Drop Week 18 | 1.551 | 0.539 | 20.68 | 0.558 |
| **50/50 EWMA + season mean** | **1.533** | **0.547** | **20.42** | **0.562** |

Lamb now projects 5.0 receptions / 67.8 yards.

Worth noting: my first test of this showed *no* improvement, because I changed
the features feeding both the model and the proxy line-setter, so they moved
together and cancelled. Testing raw predictive accuracy against actual outcomes
removed the confound. If you modify features, check what else consumes them.

## Why the edge threshold is 10% in Week 1

**Even after every correction, Week 1 calibration error is 3–5%.** The normal
edge gate is 3%. A "+4% EV" Week 1 pick is smaller than the model's own
measured error — it is not distinguishable from noise, no matter how confident
the EV number looks.

So `W1_MIN_EDGE = 0.10` and `W1_KELLY_CAP = 0.005`. That is not caution for its
own sake; it is the smallest threshold at which the claimed edge exceeds the
measured uncertainty. Expect very few picks to clear it. That is the correct
outcome.

## Exclusions applied (2026 Week 1: 440 rostered → 136 projectable)

| Excluded | n | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Changed teams in offseason | 141 | Target share was earned on another team with another QB |
| Low projected usage (<2.5 tgt) | 105 | Books barely post these; noise |
| Not on active roster | 40 | PS/IR/reserve |
| Fewer than 8 career games | 10 | No stable form |
| Injury: Out/Doubtful | 8 | From the nflverse injury report |

Questionable players are **not** excluded — they're flagged. Their risk is
volume uncertainty the model can't price, which the stake cap handles.

The team-change exclusion is aggressive and will drop players you want (any
receiver on a new team). That's deliberate for Week 1: their strongest features
describe a situation that no longer exists. Revisit from Week 3, once
current-season usage exists.

## Recommendation

Run it, log the picks, **don't bet them**. Week 1 is the single best week to
collect CLV data and the single worst week to act on it. By Week 4 you'll have
current-season usage, defence ratings built on this year's personnel, and
calibration error back near 1%.
